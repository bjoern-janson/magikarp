# Representational Governance

## Core problem

> **How should representations acquire, retain, relinquish, and revise authority under changing conditions?**

Representational Governance is the umbrella problem above MAGIKARP and CARP.

It is intentionally broader than scientific truth correction because representations can be evaluated under different warrant functions.

Examples:

- scientific mechanisms: causal evidence, intervention, replication;
- AI policies/models: evaluation, consequences, transfer;
- personal taxonomies: predictive usefulness, consistency, compression;
- organizational models: decision quality under objectives and constraints.

The warrant functions differ. The governance discipline generalizes.

## Portable principle

> **Representations should retain authority only to the extent that they continue to earn it under tests relevant to their domain.**

The framework does not claim all domains share the same authority criterion.

It claims that representations can fail structurally when:

\[
\text{representation acquires authority}
\rightarrow
\text{conditions change}
\rightarrow
\text{representation fails}
\rightarrow
\text{authority persists without adequate revalidation}
\]

## Adequacy sensitivity

A scalar confidence output is not required.

The deeper requirement is that a representation retain some pathway by which failures can become evidence about its own limits.

Healthy pattern:

\[
\text{prediction failure}
\rightarrow
\text{representation evaluation}
\rightarrow
\text{possible revision}
\]

Self-sealing pattern:

\[
\text{prediction failure}
\rightarrow
\text{reinterpretation through same representation}
\rightarrow
\text{representation protected}
\]

The issue is not that useful systems have exceptions. The issue is whether exceptions can ever accumulate enough authority to reopen the representation itself.

## Representation self-diagnosis

`SD(R)` is the candidate measurable construct pursued by MAGIKARP v0.1.

Informally:

> Can the system distinguish a failure that should be repaired within the current representation from a failure indicating that the representation itself is inadequate?

This yields a minimal hierarchy:

\[
\text{parameter error}
\neq
\text{model error}
\neq
\text{interface error}
\]

A healthy system should calibrate revision depth to failure depth.

## Self-application

Representational Governance should not maximize its own revisability.

Instead:

> **The framework should preserve identifiable conditions under which its own revision becomes warranted.**

Relevant reopenability conditions include:

- failed predictions,
- benchmark null results,
- identifiability failures,
- stronger competing explanations,
- transfer failures,
- implementation evidence showing the decomposition is unnecessary.

A theory that can explain every result after the fact but cannot state what would make it contract has failed its own governance criterion.

## Public-facing summary

> **Representational Governance studies how systems preserve the ability to revise the representations by which they determine what revisions are warranted.**

MAGIKARP is a narrower empirical program inside this problem class. CARP is one candidate operational procedure.
